# Inflation: Effects and Control Measures

> [!info] Source note
> Organized from two handwritten pages. The points below follow the class notes; where a handwritten term is unclear, it is flagged for checking against the lecture.

## Summary

Inflation affects consumers, government, financial institutions, businesses, and society, but the impact varies by income and ability to adjust. The class notes emphasize poor, fixed-income, and marginalized groups as especially exposed. To control inflation, they list monetary tools (bank rate, reserve ratio, and open-market operations), fiscal measures, and other measures. Some handwritten fiscal-policy arrows are unclear or counter to standard contractionary policy, so verify them with the lecturer.

## Effects of inflation

### Consumer groups

The notes group consumers by income:

- Low income
- Middle income
- High income

They identify the following groups as especially vulnerable:

- **Poor people**
- **People on fixed incomes**
- **Marginalized people**
- **Lower-middle-income households**
- **Upper-middle-income households**

The page labels poor, fixed-income, and marginalized groups as the “most sufferer group,” and indicates “many hidden pain” for groups lower down the income scale. The broad point is that inflation reduces purchasing power, with the burden falling most heavily on people whose incomes are low or do not adjust quickly.

### Government

The notes list government gains and losses:

- **Gains:** VAT and other taxes may yield more revenue as prices rise.
- **Losses:** spending on subsidies and social support, noted as **VGD, VGF, TCB** (Bangladesh support programmes), may rise.

### Financial institutions and banks

The notes identify banks as affected, with both gains and losses, but do not specify the mechanisms on this page.

### Business sectors

The notes divide businesses into small, medium, and large firms:

- **Small businesses** are marked as among the groups that suffer most.
- **Medium businesses:** inflation can create gains from profits on existing stocks, while losses may arise when acquiring replacement/new stock at higher prices.
- **Large businesses / conglomerates:** described as major gainers in the economy and sometimes as causes of inflation.

### Society

The notes connect inflation with wider social costs:

- Increased crime and psychological pressure.
- Pressure may strain family relationships and peace.
- The notes suggest suicide rates increase; this is a class-note claim and would need evidence before being presented as an established causal conclusion.
- Lower- and lower-middle-income families are singled out as particularly affected.

### Discussion question from class

**Does inflation affect every corner of society? Explain with argument.**

A useful answer structure: explain that rising prices affect consumers, firms, government, financial institutions, and social relations, but the effects differ by income, contracts, inventory, and ability to adjust prices or wages. Give at least one example for each group and distinguish potential gains from losses.

## Measures to control inflation

The notes sort measures into monetary, fiscal, and other measures.

### Monetary measures

Taken by the **central bank**:

- **Bank rate:** raising the rate at which commercial banks borrow from the central bank can raise the cost of credit and reduce credit flow.
- **Reserve ratio:** increasing required reserves can limit banks’ capacity to lend.
- **Open-market operations:** selling securities withdraws money from the market and channels funds to the central bank.

The intended chain in the notes is: tighter credit / lower money supply → lower credit provision and aggregate demand → reduced upward pressure on prices.

### Fiscal measures

The handwritten arrows appear to recommend:

- **Income tax:** decrease (↓)
- **VAT:** decrease (↓)
- **Tariffs:** decrease (↓)
- **Subsidies:** increase (↑)
- **Excise duties:** increase (↑)

> [!warning] Check this list
> These directions are transcribed from the page, but they do not all fit a conventional anti-inflation contractionary fiscal policy. Lower taxes and higher subsidies usually support demand or reduce selected costs; higher excise duties may raise prices. Confirm the lecturer’s intended policy mix before memorizing these arrows.

### Other measures

The page lists “Direction” as another measure, but the specific meaning is unclear in the handwriting. The top margin also says **“deflation is more dangerous than inflation.”** Keep this as a lecturer’s discussion point rather than a universal rule; the risks depend on the severity and context.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Inflation** | A sustained rise in the general price level, reducing the purchasing power of money. |
| **Fixed-income group** | People whose nominal income does not adjust quickly as prices rise. |
| **Bank rate** | Central bank lending rate charged to commercial banks. |
| **Reserve ratio** | Share of deposits banks must hold as reserves rather than lend. |
| **Open-market operation** | Central bank purchase or sale of securities to influence liquidity and money supply. |

> [!note] Added standard definition
> The inflation and fixed-income definitions above are standard study definitions added for clarity; they are not fully written out on the source pages.

> [!question]- 2. How can tighter monetary policy reduce inflationary pressure?
> Higher borrowing costs or reserve requirements can reduce credit and money growth, lowering aggregate demand and easing upward pressure on prices.
