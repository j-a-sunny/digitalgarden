# Chapter 2: Company and Marketing Strategy
*Partnering to Build Customer Relationships*

> [!info] About this note
> Rewritten from the lecture slides (Pearson / Prentice Hall, 37 slides) for studying. Definitions are kept close to the slides. Memory aids and review questions are mine and are marked as such.

## The big picture (read this first)

A company plans in two layers:

1. **Corporate level:** decide who we are and what businesses we are in.
2. **Marketing level:** decide which customers we serve and how (segment, target, position, then the 4Ps).

Then marketing is **planned, implemented and controlled**.

```
Mission → Objectives & goals → Business portfolio → Marketing strategy (STP + 4Ps) → Plan → Implement → Control
```

![Ch2-slide-04.png](/img/user/Study/3rd%20Semester/Marketing/attachments/Ch2-slide-04.png)

> [!tip] Chapter in one sentence
> Strategic planning sets the company's direction, the portfolio decides which businesses get resources, and marketing strategy turns that into customer-focused action.

## 1. Companywide strategic planning

### What is strategic planning?

- A **plan** is a defined course of action.
- **Strategic planning** = developing and maintaining a **strategic fit** between the organization's goals and capabilities and its changing marketing opportunities.
- A good plan helps the company cope with changing situations.

### Steps in strategic planning

| Level | Step |
|---|---|
| Corporate level | 1. Define the company mission |
| Corporate level | 2. Set company objectives and goals |
| Corporate level | 3. Design the business portfolio |
| Business unit, product and market level | 4. Plan marketing and other functional strategies |

> [!tip] Memory aid (mine)
> **M-O-P-S**: **M**ission → **O**bjectives → **P**ortfolio → marketing **S**trategies. The first three are corporate level; only the last is at the business, product and market level.

### Defining a market-oriented mission

- **Mission statement:** the organization's purpose, what it wants to accomplish in the larger environment.
- A **market-oriented** mission defines the business by the **basic customer needs it satisfies**, not by the product it sells.
- Slide example (Google): *"We help you organize the world's information and make it universally accessible and useful."*

| Company | Product-oriented (weaker) | Market-oriented (better) |
|---|---|---|
| Amazon.com | We sell books, videos, CDs, toys, consumer electronics and other products online | We make the Internet buying experience fast, easy and enjoyable; we're the place where you can find and discover anything you want to buy online |
| Disney | We run theme parks | We create fantasies, a place where dreams come true |
| Nike | We sell athletic shoes and apparel | We bring inspiration and innovation to every athlete in the world (*if you have a body, you are an athlete*) |

> [!tip] How to spot the difference
> Product-oriented = **what we sell** ("we sell shoes"). Market-oriented = **what need or experience we deliver** ("inspiration and innovation").

### Setting company objectives and goals

| Business objectives | Marketing objectives |
|---|---|
| Build profitable customer relationships | Increase market share |
| Invest in research | Create local partnerships |
| Improve profits | Increase promotion |

Business objectives are company-wide. Marketing objectives support them.

## 2. Designing the business portfolio

- **Business portfolio:** the collection of businesses and products that make up the company.
- **Portfolio analysis:** management evaluates those products and businesses. It is a major activity in strategic planning.

> [!example] Local example: Beximco Group
> A diversified portfolio across many industries:
> - **Pharmaceuticals:** Beximco Pharmaceuticals, Bangladesh Antibiotics Industries, Pharmatek Chemicals
> - **Textile:** Bextex, Beximco Fashions, Beximco Apparels, International Knitwear & Apparels, Crescent Fashions, Essess Fashions
> - **Real estate and construction:** Bangladesh Export Import Co., Beximco Engineering, Beximco Property Development & Management
> - **Ceramics:** Shinepukur Ceramics
> - **ICT and media:** Bangladesh Online, Beximco Computers, Beximco Media
> - **Synthetics:** Beximco Synthetics
> - **Aviation:** GMG Airlines
> - **Energy:** Beximco Petroleum
> - **Investment and financial services:** Beximco Holdings, Beximco Securities
> - **Jute:** New Dacca Industries, Sonali Ansh
> - **Travel services:** Bextrade

### Strategic business unit (SBU)

A unit of the company with its **own mission and objectives** that can be planned **separately** from the rest. It can be:

- a company division
- a product line within a division
- a single product or brand

### Analyzing the current portfolio: 3 steps

1. **Identify** the key businesses (SBUs).
2. **Assess** how attractive each SBU is.
3. **Decide** how much support each SBU deserves.

### Growth-share matrix (slide 12)

![Ch2-slide-12.png](/img/user/Study/3rd%20Semester/Marketing/attachments/Ch2-slide-12.png)

The slide plots **market growth rate** (vertical) against **relative market share** (horizontal), using product pictures as examples.

| | High relative share | Low relative share |
|---|---|---|
| **High market growth** | Air conditioner (picture) | UPS / power backup (picture) |
| **Low market growth** | Cow (picture) | Audio cassette (picture) |

> [!note] Check against your class notes
> The slide does not print the quadrant names. In the standard BCG matrix they are: high growth + high share = **Star**, high growth + low share = **Question mark**, low growth + high share = **Cash cow**, low growth + low share = **Dog**.

### Problems with matrix approaches

> [!warning] Four drawbacks (easy marks)
> 1. Hard to **define SBUs** and **measure market share and growth**
> 2. **Time consuming**
> 3. **Expensive**
> 4. Focus on **current** businesses, not **future** planning

## 3. Strategies for growth and downsizing

The **product/market expansion grid** identifies growth opportunities.

![Ch2-slide-16.png](/img/user/Study/3rd%20Semester/Marketing/attachments/Ch2-slide-16.png)

| | Existing products | New products |
|---|---|---|
| **Existing markets** | **Market penetration** | **Product development** |
| **New markets** | **Market development** | **Diversification** |

> [!tip] Quick way to tell them apart (mine)
> Ask two questions: *Is the product new? Is the market new?*
> - Neither new → **penetration**
> - Only market new → **market development**
> - Only product new → **product development**
> - Both new → **diversification** (riskiest, furthest from what the company already knows)

| Strategy | What it means |
|---|---|
| Market penetration | Increase sales to current segments **without changing the product** |
| Market development | Find and develop **new segments** for **current products** |
| Product development | Offer **new or modified products** to **existing segments** |
| Diversification | Start up or acquire businesses **outside current products and markets** |

### Downsizing

**Downsizing** = reducing the portfolio by eliminating products or units that are **not profitable** or **no longer fit the overall strategy**.

Why a company downsizes:

1. The product or market is **less profitable**.
2. The firm **grew too fast**.
3. It entered an area where it **lacks experience**.
4. The **market environment** changed.
5. Some products or units simply **age and die**.

## 4. Planning marketing: partnering to build customer relationships

The relationship goal is to **attract**, **retain**, and **grow** customers. The slides also mention **friend relationship management** and **partner relationship management**.

### Partnering with other departments: the value chain

**Value chain:** a series of departments that carry out value-creating activities to design, produce, market, deliver and support a firm's products.

![Ch2-slide-21.png](/img/user/Study/3rd%20Semester/Marketing/attachments/Ch2-slide-21.png)

| Type | Activities |
|---|---|
| **Primary** (left to right) | Inbound logistics → Operations → Outbound logistics → Marketing & sales → Service |
| **Support** | Firm infrastructure, Human resource management, Technology development, Procurement |

Both feed into the **margin**. Marketing is only one link, so it must work with the other departments.

### Partnering with others: the value delivery network

**Value delivery network** = the **company + suppliers + distributors + customers**, partnering to improve the performance of the **entire system**. (Slide example: Toyota's supplier website.)

> [!tip] Don't mix these up
> **Value chain** = *inside* the company (departments). **Value delivery network** = *outside* partners as well.

## 5. Marketing strategy and the marketing mix

### Customer-driven strategy: segment, target, position

| Step | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Market segmentation** | Dividing a market into distinct groups of buyers with distinct needs, characteristics or behavior, who might need separate products or marketing mixes |
| *(Market segment)* | A group of consumers who respond in a similar way to a given set of marketing efforts |
| **Target marketing** | Evaluating each segment's attractiveness and selecting one or more to enter |
| **Market positioning** | Arranging for a product to occupy a clear, distinctive and desirable place relative to competitors in the minds of target consumers |

> [!tip] Memory aid (mine)
> **S → T → P**: *Divide* the market, *choose* who to serve, *decide* how they should see you.

### The marketing mix (4Ps)

**Marketing mix:** the set of **controllable** tactical marketing tools (product, price, place, promotion) that the firm blends to produce the response it wants in the **target market**.

![Ch2-slide-28.png](/img/user/Study/3rd%20Semester/Marketing/attachments/Ch2-slide-28.png)

| Product | Price | Place | Promotion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Variety | List price | Channels | Advertising |
| Quality | Discounts | Coverage | Personal selling |
| Design | Allowances | Assortments | Sales promotion |
| Features | Payment period | Locations | Public relations |
| Brand name | Credit terms | Inventory | |
| Packaging | | Transportation | |
| Services | | Logistics | |

All four point at the **target customers** and the **intended positioning**.

### 4Ps vs 4Cs

| 4Ps (seller's view) | 4Cs (customer's view) |
|---|---|
| Product | Customer solution |
| Price | Customer cost |
| Place | Convenience |
| Promotion | Communication |

## 6. Managing the marketing effort

### SWOT analysis

![Ch2-slide-31.png](/img/user/Study/3rd%20Semester/Marketing/attachments/Ch2-slide-31.png)

| | Positive | Negative |
|---|---|---|
| **Internal** | Strengths | Weaknesses |
| **External** | Opportunities | Threats |

> [!note]
> The slide only labels internal/external and positive/negative; the SWOT names are the standard reading of that grid.

### Parts of a marketing plan

1. Executive summary
2. Marketing situation
3. Threats and opportunities
4. Objectives and issues
5. Action programs
6. Budgets
7. Controls

> [!tip] Memory aid (mine)
> **E-S-T-O-A-B-C**: Executive summary, Situation, Threats/opportunities, Objectives, Action programs, Budgets, Controls.

### Marketing implementation

**Implementing** turns marketing plans into marketing actions that accomplish the strategic marketing objectives. It succeeds when the company blends these into one action plan that supports its strategies:

- **people**
- **organizational structure**
- **decision and reward system**
- **company culture**

### Marketing department organization

![Ch2-slide-34.png](/img/user/Study/3rd%20Semester/Marketing/attachments/Ch2-slide-34.png)

1. **Functional** organization
2. **Geographic** organization
3. **Product management** organization
4. **Market or customer management**

### Marketing control

**Controlling** = measuring and evaluating results and taking corrective action as needed. Two types:

- **Operating control**
- **Strategic control**

### Marketing ROI

**Return on marketing investment** = the net return from a marketing investment divided by its cost. It measures the profit generated by marketing activities.

$$
\text{Marketing ROI} = \frac{\text{Net return from marketing investment}}{\text{Cost of marketing investment}}
$$

## Self-test questions

> [!question]- 1. What is strategic planning, and what does "strategic fit" mean?
> Developing and maintaining a fit between the organization's goals and capabilities and its changing marketing opportunities.

> [!question]- 2. List the four steps of strategic planning.
> Define the company mission → set company objectives and goals → design the business portfolio → plan marketing and other functional strategies.

> [!question]- 3. Why is a market-oriented mission better than a product-oriented one? Give an example.
> It defines the business by the customer need it satisfies. Nike: "We sell athletic shoes" (product) vs "We bring inspiration and innovation to every athlete in the world" (market).

> [!question]- 4. What is an SBU? Give its three possible forms.
> A unit with a separate mission and objectives, planned separately. It can be a company division, a product line within a division, or a single product or brand.

> [!question]- 5. State the three steps in analyzing the current business portfolio.
> Identify the key SBUs, assess their attractiveness, decide how much support each deserves.

> [!question]- 6. Give four problems with matrix approaches.
> Hard to define SBUs and measure share and growth; time consuming; expensive; focus on current businesses rather than future planning.

> [!question]- 7. Explain the four strategies of the product/market expansion grid.
> Penetration (current segments, same product), market development (new segments, current products), product development (new or modified products, existing segments), diversification (new businesses outside current products and markets).

> [!question]- 8. When and why do companies downsize?
> To drop unprofitable or poorly fitting units. Reasons: less profitable product or market, grew too fast, entered an unfamiliar area, market environment changed, products or units aged and died.

> [!question]- 9. Distinguish the value chain from the value delivery network.
> Value chain: internal departments that create value (primary and support activities). Value delivery network: the company plus suppliers, distributors and customers working together to improve the whole system.

> [!question]- 10. Define segmentation, targeting and positioning.
> Segmentation divides the market into distinct buyer groups. Targeting evaluates segments and picks one or more. Positioning gives the product a clear, distinctive, desirable place in consumers' minds relative to competitors.

> [!question]- 11. What are the 4Ps and the matching 4Cs?
> Product / customer solution, price / customer cost, place / convenience, promotion / communication.

> [!question]- 12. What are the parts of a marketing plan?
> Executive summary, marketing situation, threats and opportunities, objectives and issues, action programs, budgets, controls.

> [!question]- 13. What does successful implementation depend on?
> How well the company blends people, organizational structure, decision and reward system, and company culture into one action plan.

> [!question]- 14. List the four ways of organizing a marketing department.
> Functional, geographic, product management, market or customer management.

> [!question]- 15. Define marketing ROI.
> Net return from a marketing investment divided by the cost of the investment; it measures the profits generated by marketing activities.
