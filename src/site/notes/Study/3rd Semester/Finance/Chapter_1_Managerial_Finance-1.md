# WHAT IS FINANCE?

Finance is concerned with the acquisition, allocation, and management of funds. It involves raising funds, investing funds, managing financial resources, and dealing with financial risk.

A simple finance process can be explained as: Raise Funds → Invest Funds → Generate Cash Flows.

Finance also requires consideration of three fundamental elements: return, risk, and time.

# MAJOR AREAS OF FINANCE

## Financial Markets and Institutions

This area studies banks, stock markets, bond markets, financial intermediaries, interest rates, and financial instruments. Financial markets help transfer funds from savers to borrowers.

Basic flow: Savers → Financial Markets and Institutions → Borrowers and Businesses.

## Investments

Investment finance focuses on selecting financial assets and evaluating expected returns and risks. Examples include shares, bonds, mutual funds, and government securities.

Key question: Where should an investor place funds to obtain an acceptable return for a given level of risk?

## Financial Services

Financial services include banking, insurance, investment management, financial planning, and brokerage services.

## Managerial Finance

Managerial finance focuses on financial decision-making within organizations. A financial manager must determine what assets to invest in, how to obtain funds, and how to manage day-to-day financial activities.

# FINANCE AND OTHER BUSINESS FUNCTIONS

## Finance and Management

Management decisions regarding expansion, production, human resources, and strategy have financial consequences. For example, when a production manager proposes buying a machine, finance evaluates its cost, expected benefits, cash flows, risk, and financing requirements.

## Finance and Marketing

Marketing decisions require financial resources. A financial manager may evaluate whether expenditure on advertising, promotion, discounts, or market expansion is justified by the expected increase in sales and cash flows.

## Finance and Accounting

Accounting primarily records and reports what has happened. Finance uses financial information to support future-oriented decisions. Accounting may report that profit was Tk. 5 million, while finance asks whether the profit generated sufficient cash and how future performance can be improved.

## Finance and Economics

Finance relies on economic concepts such as demand and supply, inflation, interest rates, economic growth, and exchange rates. Changes in the economic environment directly affect financial decisions.

# ROLE OF THE FINANCIAL MANAGER

- Financial planning and forecasting
- Investment analysis and capital budgeting
- Financing decisions
- Cash and working capital management
- Risk management
- Dividend decisions
- Financial performance evaluation

In large corporations, financial activities are generally supervised by the Chief Financial Officer (CFO). The treasurer is commonly associated with raising and managing funds, while the controller is commonly associated with accounting, reporting, and internal control.

# FORMS OF BUSINESS ORGANIZATION

## Sole Proprietorship

A sole proprietorship is owned by one individual. Its advantages include ease of establishment, direct control, and simple decision-making. Major disadvantages include limited access to capital, limited continuity, and unlimited liability.

## Partnership

A partnership is owned by two or more persons. It provides additional capital and combines skills and responsibilities. Potential disadvantages include conflicts among partners, shared profits, and liability issues.

## Corporation

A corporation is a separate legal entity owned by shareholders. Major advantages include limited liability, transferability of ownership, perpetual life, and greater access to capital. The separation of ownership and management can, however, create agency problems.

# THE GOAL OF FINANCIAL MANAGEMENT

The central objective of financial management is generally expressed as maximizing the value of the firm. For publicly traded corporations, this is closely associated with maximizing shareholder wealth.

Profit maximization alone is insufficient because financial decisions must also consider the timing of returns, risk, and cash flows.

# WHY PROFIT MAXIMIZATION IS NOT SUFFICIENT

## Timing

Tk. 1 million received today is generally more valuable than Tk. 1 million received five years later because money available today can be invested. This leads to the concept of the time value of money.

## Risk

Two projects may offer different expected profits but also different levels of uncertainty. Therefore, profit alone cannot determine the quality of an investment decision.

## Cash Flow

A company may report accounting profit without having sufficient cash available. For example, sales made on credit may increase reported profit while customers have not yet paid. Consequently, a profitable company may still face liquidity problems.

# THREE MAJOR FINANCIAL DECISIONS

## Investment Decision

The investment decision concerns where the firm should invest its funds. Examples include purchasing machinery, constructing a factory, introducing a new product, or investing in technology. Long-term investment decisions are commonly called capital budgeting decisions.

## Financing Decision

The financing decision concerns how the firm will obtain funds. Major sources include debt, such as bank loans and bonds, and equity, such as common shares and retained earnings. The combination of debt and equity is called the capital structure.

## Working Capital Management

Working capital management concerns the management of short-term assets and liabilities. Current assets include cash, inventory, and accounts receivable. Current liabilities include accounts payable and short-term borrowings.

Net Working Capital = Current Assets − Current Liabilities.

# RISK AND RETURN

One of the most important principles in finance is the relationship between risk and return. Generally, investors require a higher expected return for accepting greater risk. However, higher risk does not guarantee a higher actual return.

Conceptual relationship: Higher Risk → Higher Required Return.

# FINANCIAL MARKETS AND INSTITUTIONS

Financial markets facilitate the transfer of funds between those with surplus funds and those who need funds. Major examples include money markets, capital markets, stock markets, and bond markets.

## Primary Market

The primary market is where new securities are issued and firms raise new capital.

## Secondary Market

The secondary market is where existing securities are traded among investors.

# AGENCY RELATIONSHIP AND AGENCY PROBLEM

In a corporation, shareholders are the principals because they own the company, while managers are agents because they are hired to operate the business. An agency problem occurs when managers make decisions that serve their own interests rather than the interests of shareholders.

- Performance-based compensation
- Managerial stock ownership
- Monitoring by the board of directors
- Independent auditing
- Corporate governance mechanisms

# BUSINESS ETHICS

Ethics refers to standards of right and wrong behavior. Financial managers should consider honesty, fairness, transparency, responsibility, and accountability. Unethical decisions may generate short-term benefits but can result in legal penalties, reputational damage, and long-term financial losses.
