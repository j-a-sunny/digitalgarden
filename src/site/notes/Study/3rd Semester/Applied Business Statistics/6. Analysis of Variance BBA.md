> [!summary] Summary
> ANOVA (developed by R.A. Fisher) splits the **total variation** in data into a part explained by an **assignable cause** (the factor being studied) and a part due to **chance** (error). It tests whether **more than two means** are equal using an $F$ ratio: $F=\dfrac{\text{variation between groups}}{\text{variation within groups}}$. If $F_{calc}>F_{table}$, at least one mean differs. **One-way** uses one factor; **two-way** uses two.

## Why ANOVA instead of many $t$ tests
- Comparing $k$ means pair by pair needs many $t$ tests.
- Many tests increase the overall chance of a **Type I error**.
- ANOVA tests all means at once.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Assignable cause | Variation that can be detected and measured separately |
| Chance cause | Natural variation beyond control and not explained by the assignable causes |
| Response (yield) | The quantity measured on an experimental unit after a treatment (paddy yield per plot, weight gain of an animal, a student's IQ) |
| Factor | An explanatory (independent) variable manipulated by the experimenter |
| Level | One value or category of a factor |
| One-way classification | Observations grouped by a single factor |
| Two-way classification | Observations grouped by two factors |

## Assumptions
1. Samples come from **normal** populations.
2. All populations have a **common variance**.
3. Samples are **independent** and identically distributed.
4. The effects of factors are **additive**.

If these cannot be assumed, use a non-parametric technique instead (see [[Study/3rd Semester/Applied Business Statistics/7. Nonparametric Methods 06052021\|7. Nonparametric Methods 06052021]]).

## One-way classification

$n$ observations in $k$ classes with $r$ observations each. $y_{ij}$ is the $j$th value in class $i$ ($i=1..k$, $j=1..r$), $N=kr$.

**Model:** $y_{ij}=\mu+\alpha_i+\varepsilon_{ij}$ where $\mu$ = general mean, $\alpha_i$ = effect of level $i$, $\varepsilon_{ij}$ = random error.

**Hypotheses:** $H_0:\mu_1=\mu_2=\dots=\mu_k$ vs $H_1$: not all $\mu_i$ are equal (at least one differs).

### Partition of the total sum of squares
$$\sum_{i}\sum_{j}(y_{ij}-\bar y_{..})^2=r\sum_i(\bar y_{i.}-\bar y_{..})^2+\sum_i\sum_j(y_{ij}-\bar y_{i.})^2$$

**Total SS = Between-classes SS + Within-classes (error) SS.** The cross-product term vanishes because deviations inside a class sum to zero.

Computing formulas (with $y_{..}$ = grand total):
$$SS_T=\sum\sum y_{ij}^2-\frac{y_{..}^2}{N},\qquad SS_B=\frac1r\sum_i y_{i.}^2-\frac{y_{..}^2}{N},\qquad SS_W=SS_T-SS_B$$

### One-way ANOVA table

| Source | SS | d.f. | Mean square | $F$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Between classes | $SS_B$ | $k-1$ | $MS_B=SS_B/(k-1)$ | $F=MS_B/MSE$ |
| Error (within) | $SS_W$ | $N-k$ | $MSE=SS_W/(N-k)$ | |
| Total | $SS_T$ | $N-1$ | | |

$F$ has $(k-1,\ N-k)$ d.f.

> [!warning] Slide error
> The table writes the $F$ degrees of freedom as "$(k-1)(N-k)$". They are a **pair**: $(k-1,\ N-k)$, numerator d.f. first. Worked example: [[Study/3rd Semester/Applied Business Statistics/ANOVA one way classification and two way classification\|ANOVA one way classification and two way classification]] uses $(3,8)$ and $(3,12)$.

**Decision:** reject $H_0$ if $F_{calc}>F_{table}$; otherwise fail to reject.

## Two-way classification

Factor A has $a$ levels (rows) and factor B has $b$ levels (columns), one observation per cell, $N=ab$.

**Model:** $y_{ij}=\mu+\tau_i+\beta_j+\varepsilon_{ij}$ with $\tau_i$ = row-factor effect, $\beta_j$ = column-factor (block) effect, $\varepsilon_{ij}\sim NID(0,\sigma^2)$.

**Partition:** $SS_T=SS_A+SS_B+SS_E$ (cross-products vanish).

$$SS_T=\sum\sum y_{ij}^2-\frac{y_{..}^2}{N},\quad SS_A=\frac1b\sum_i y_{i.}^2-\frac{y_{..}^2}{N},\quad SS_B=\frac1a\sum_j y_{.j}^2-\frac{y_{..}^2}{N},\quad SS_E=SS_T-SS_A-SS_B$$

**Two sets of hypotheses:** row means equal ($H_0:\mu_1=\dots=\mu_a$) and column means equal ($H_0:\mu_1=\dots=\mu_b$).

| Source | SS | d.f. | MS | $F$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Between levels of A | $SS_A$ | $a-1$ | $MS_A=SS_A/(a-1)$ | $MS_A/MSE$ with $(a-1,\ (a-1)(b-1))$ d.f. |
| Between levels of B | $SS_B$ | $b-1$ | $MS_B=SS_B/(b-1)$ | $MS_B/MSE$ with $(b-1,\ (a-1)(b-1))$ d.f. |
| Error | $SS_E$ | $(a-1)(b-1)$ | $MSE=SS_E/[(a-1)(b-1)]$ | |
| Total | $SS_T$ | $N-1$ | | |

## After rejecting $H_0$: multiple comparisons
A rejected $H_0$ says *some* means differ, not *which*. Pair-wise comparison methods on the slides: **Tukey's test** and **Fisher's Least Significant Difference (LSD)**.

**LSD method.** For each pair test $H_0:\mu_i=\mu_j$ with
$$t_0=\frac{\bar y_{i.}-\bar y_{j.}}{\sqrt{MSE\left(\tfrac1{n_i}+\tfrac1{n_j}\right)}},\qquad LSD=t_{\alpha/2,\,N-a}\sqrt{MSE\left(\tfrac1{n_i}+\tfrac1{n_j}\right)}$$
For a balanced design: $LSD=t_{\alpha/2,\,N-a}\sqrt{\dfrac{2\,MSE}{n}}$. Any pair of treatment averages differing by more than the LSD is significantly different.

> [!note] Notation on the slide
> The slide's "$N-a$" uses $a$ for the number of treatments (what the one-way table calls $k$). The error d.f. there is $N-k$.

> [!tip] Added: memory aids
> - **"Between over within."** $F$ = how far apart the group means are divided by how noisy each group is. A large $F$ means the group means are spread out more than chance would explain.
> - Identity to remember: **Total = Between + Within** (one-way), **Total = A + B + Error** (two-way).
> - One-way error d.f. $=N-k$. Two-way error d.f. $=(a-1)(b-1)$.
> - ANOVA $F$ test is **one-tailed** (right tail only).

> [!question]- Self-test 1: Why use ANOVA rather than several $t$ tests?
> Several tests inflate the overall Type I error rate and need many more calculations.

> $k-1=4$ and $N-k=30-5=25$, so $F$ with $(4,25)$ d.f.

> Normal populations, common variance, independent samples, additive effects.

> $(4-1)(3-1)=6$.
