# BNCC Naval Wing: B Certificate Prep

> [!abstract] Quick revision map
> Start with the syllabus and practical instructions your flotilla gave you. For a fast review, work through **seamanship and knots**, **boat/ship terminology**, **naval orientation and 1971 history**, then the common BNCC topics (drill, map reading, first aid, and weapon safety) that your instructors assigned.
>
> **Important:** I could not verify a public, complete BNCC Naval B Certificate syllabus or official marks distribution. The India NCC mark table sometimes circulated online is not evidence of BNCC exam weighting. Treat all topic priority here as a revision suggestion, not a prediction of marks.

## 1. Seamanship: knots, bends, and hitches

| Knot                            | What it does                                                        | Remember                                                               |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Bowline                         | Makes a fixed loop that normally does not tighten under load        | A loop that stays a loop                                               |
| Sheet bend                      | Joins two ropes, especially ropes of different sizes                | Use a double sheet bend for a more secure hold when needed             |
| Reef (square) knot              | Ties two ends of the same rope together for light, non-critical use | Not for joining loaded ropes; it can capsize or slip                   |
| Clove hitch                     | Fast temporary fastening around a post or spar; useful in lashings  | Can loosen under changing load; secure the ends as taught              |
| Figure-eight                    | Stopper knot that helps prevent a rope running through an opening   | Easy to recognize by its shape                                         |
| Sheepshank                      | Temporarily shortens a rope without cutting it                      | Not a substitute for a permanent shortening method under critical load |
| Timber hitch                    | Grips a spar or log for hauling                                     | Keep the turns snug and in the correct direction                       |
| Round turn and two half hitches | Secures a rope to a ring, post, or spar                             | The round turn takes strain; the half hitches secure it                |
| Anchor (fisherman's) bend       | Secures a rope to an anchor ring or similar fitting                 | Exact form and naming may vary by course                               |

> [!warning] Reef knot safety
> A reef knot is for tying a reef in a sail or bundling light items. It is **not** a safe bend for joining two loaded ropes. If asked to join ropes, give the sheet bend and explain why.

> [!tip] Quick recall *(study aid added)*
> **Bowline = loop. Sheet bend = join. Hitch = attach. Figure-eight = stop. Sheepshank = shorten.** Say the purpose before demonstrating the knot, then check that the finished knot is dressed and snug.

### Knot practice checklist

1. Name the knot and state its use before tying.
2. Tie it slowly with the standing part and working end identified.
3. Dress the knot so the turns lie neatly; remove slack where appropriate.
4. Explain one limitation or safety point, especially for the reef knot and temporary hitches.
5. Ask your instructor to check your method against the course standard.

## 2. Boat and ship vocabulary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Bow / stern | Front / rear of a boat or ship |
| Port / starboard | Left / right when facing forward |
| Fore / aft | Toward the bow / toward the stern |
| Amidships | The middle area of a vessel |
| Keel | Main structural member along the bottom of a boat |
| Gunwale | Upper edge of a boat's side |
| Thwart | Cross-seat in a small boat; may also help brace its sides |
| Rudder / tiller | Steering surface / handle or bar used to move it |
| Oar / blade / loom / handle | Whole rowing implement / broad end in the water / shaft / end held by the rower |

> [!note] Lights and sides
> Port is the left side facing forward and is conventionally marked red; starboard is the right side and is conventionally marked green. Learn the instructor's exact wording for rowing commands; don't rely on a generic internet list.

## 3. Naval communication and orientation

- **Phonetic alphabet:** know the standard sequence from Alpha through Zulu if included in your class material.
- **Visual signalling:** revise the methods actually taught (for example, flags or semaphore) and practise sending and receiving, not just naming them.
- **Navigation basics:** if covered, review compass points, bearings, map/chart symbols, scale, and how to follow an instructor's practical demonstration.
- **Naval customs:** learn the local course's rules for saluting, reporting, and conduct around a quarterdeck or boat. Customs and wording should be confirmed with your instructor.

## 4. Bangladesh Navy and the Liberation War

### Bangladesh Navy in 1971

- The Bangladesh Navy traces its wartime origins to 1971. For the exact formal-establishment date and event expected in your course, use the wording in your BNCC handout.
- The Navy motto is **“In War and Peace Invincible at Sea.”**
- **Operation Jackpot:** naval commandos attacked shipping at several ports and waterways using limpet mines. The Bangladesh Navy history account says 26 enemy ships were destroyed or damaged and describes the operation as disrupting shipping and port activity. Different accounts describe the date and the number sunk differently; use your teacher's formulation in an exam.
- **Sector 10:** associated with naval commando operations and the coastal/waterway front. The sector's operational reach is described differently across summaries, so follow your class source.
- **Padma and Palash:** these gunboats were involved in the December 1971 operation toward Chalna; the Navy history says they came under air attack near Khulna Shipyard on 10 December. Avoid calling this “Operation Hotpants” unless that exact name appears in your handout; I could not substantiate that label in the official Navy history.
- **Bir Sreshtho Mohammad Ruhul Amin:** the Navy's recipient of the highest gallantry award *(confirm the spelling and expected detail from your class material)*.

### Useful viva answer pattern

For a historical operation, answer in four parts: **name → date/period → objective or location → result/significance**. If you cannot remember a disputed detail, state the reliable core fact clearly rather than guessing a number.

## 6. BNCC and Naval Wing facts to confirm

- The Naval Wing has flotillas **51 (Dhaka), 52 (Chattogram), and 53 (Khulna)** in the overview currently linked from [[BNCC/BNCC draft\|BNCC draft]]. Treat any proposed or newly announced unit as time-sensitive.
- The cadet-rank list previously copied into [[BNCC/BNCC draft\|BNCC draft]] appears to mix regular Bangladesh Navy sailor ranks with BNCC cadet titles. A commonly reported cadet progression is **Cadet → Cadet Lance Corporal → Cadet Corporal → Cadet Sergeant → Cadet Under Officer**. Confirm the official titles and insignia with your instructor before memorizing; the public source material is not clear enough to treat this as definitive.
- Do not use the regular Navy sailor ladder (for example, Ordinary Seaman → Able Seaman → Petty Officer) as a BNCC cadet ladder unless your instructor explicitly teaches it as a comparison.
- Commissioned officers' ranks differ by service. Avoid describing a Naval Wing rank as “Lieutenant Colonel equivalent”; that is an Army rank. Learn only the officer titles actually required by your handout.

## 7. Common subjects to cross-check in your handout

Use this as a checklist, not as a claim that every topic appears in the B Certificate exam:

- Foot drill and arms drill; correct words of command and turnout.
- Map reading, conventional signs, grid references, scale, and compass bearings.
- Weapon knowledge and range safety, limited to the exact training weapon and procedures taught by authorized instructors.
- First aid, hygiene, and basic disaster response.
- BNCC organization, wing structure, cadet responsibilities, and national history.
- Swimming, boat pulling, modelling, or other practical items only if your syllabus lists them.

> [!warning] Practical safety
> Do not practise weapon handling from written instructions alone. Follow the supervised procedure and safety commands taught by your authorized BNCC instructor.

## 8. Self-test

> [!question]- Which knot makes a fixed loop?
> Bowline.

> [!question]- Which bend is used to join ropes of different sizes?
> Sheet bend; a double sheet bend can provide a more secure version when appropriate.

> [!question]- Why is a reef knot unsuitable for joining two loaded ropes?
> It may capsize or slip under load. It is for light, non-critical use.

> [!question]- Which knot is commonly used as a stopper?
> Figure-eight knot.

> [!question]- Which knot temporarily shortens a rope?
> Sheepshank.

> [!question]- What are port and starboard?
> Port is left and starboard is right when facing forward; their conventional navigation light colours are red and green respectively.

> [!question]- What is the purpose of a round turn and two half hitches?
> To secure a rope to a ring, post, or spar, with the round turn taking strain and the half hitches securing it.

> [!question]- What was Operation Jackpot?
> A series of naval commando attacks on shipping and port activity during the 1971 Liberation War, including attacks using limpet mines.

> [!question]- What source should decide exact exam wording, marks, and practical commands?
> The current BNCC handout and instructor for your flotilla.
